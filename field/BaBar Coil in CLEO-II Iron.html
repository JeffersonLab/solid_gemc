BaBar coil in CLEO-II iron

The BaBar solenoid coil and its aluminium cryostat in CLEO-II iron adapted from CLEOv10 (see “Iron changes”). BABARv10 has the 1995 TDR coil (5.119 MA-turns at 7110 A); BABARv11 has the coil as built (4.904 MA-turns at 4596 A). Both are compared with CLEOv10 (the CLEO coil) and with the BaBar papers.

BABARv10: the coil, taken from the sol_sim page (Gen's babar_transv_2.am), is the coil of the BaBar Technical Design Report, SLAC-R-457 (1995), chapter 9. Its Table 9-3 gives a winding 3456 mm long with mean radius 1532 mm, 5.1192 MA-turns at 7110 A (0.985 H, 25 MJ). The text below the table splits it into three regions in series: a centre of 1728 mm and two ends of 864 mm, with the same number of turns in each, so the ends carry 2× the centre current density. One layer; Table 9-4 gives the conductor as 3.2 / 6.8 mm bare and 3.6 / 7.2 mm insulated (0.4 mm insulation per turn), and those pitches fill the sections exactly (864 mm / turns = 3.6 mm, 1728 mm / turns = 7.2 mm).

BABARv11 has the coil as built (Bell 1999, O'Connor 1999; see “Against the BaBar references”): 1067 turns at 4596 A, 4.904 MA-turns, 3 regions in series in one block r 150.30–154.30 cm for the 2 layers, 349.68 cm long: the room-temperature size shrunk to 4.5 K with the IEEE 1996 design's 0.43 %. The section split is an assumption, since no reference gives it: pitch = conductor + 0.5 mm insulation per turn (the estimate table below), with the same transition z in both layers. End / centre J = 1.66. Iron and cryostat are identical to BABARv10.

The cryostat is a vacuum vessel: an aluminium support cylinder, heat shield and housing around evacuated spaces, about 12.6 cm of aluminium in total. POISSON treats aluminium and vacuum alike (μ = 1, no current), so the solid cryostat region gives the same field and forces as a hollow one.

The as-built coil

Bell et al. 1999 (Table 1) and O'Connor et al. 1999 (Table I, “as built”) agree: 2 layers and 1067 turns. Current: “4596 A operating (4605 A gave 1.503 T)” (Bell); “4596 A operating; 4650 A design (from the packing factor of the wound coil); design current 4825 A = 1.05 × nominal” (O'Connor). Both say the current density is graded “by using conductor of two different thickness”: thick (8.49 mm) in the centre, thin (4.93 mm) at the ends, 20 mm radial (Table II sizes; the text rounds them to 8.4 and 5 mm). It is one series circuit, so every turn carries the same current; O'Connor's Table IV names the three sectors of the inner layer “thin forward”, “thick middle” and “thin backward”.

A 2022 JLab engineering risk assessment of the magnet (Ghoshal 2022) reprints O'Connor's as-built table and Table IV, with no section lengths. It adds one later measurement: in the 2018 BNL high-field test (sPHENIX iron), the central field was “about 7% lower than calculated”, which it attributes to “a combination of issues”: 40-year-old steel, “actual number of turns in the magnet coil being 1 to 2 % lower than what was used in the model” and larger than expected gaps in the steel.

No reference found gives the section lengths of the as-built coil (the turns of thin and thick conductor, or where they meet), nor whether the two layers change conductor at the same z. A model of the as-built coil therefore needs an assumption about the insulation per turn; the estimate table below shows how the split depends on it. Two indirect hints favour the longer ends: the 1997 technical specification (UCRL-ID-126263, 5.3.3) says “The conductor turn-to-turn insulation shall not be less than 0.5 mm total thickness” (a minimum for the vendor, not the as-built value), and the ePHENIX and sPHENIX proposals say the 5 mm conductor is used “for the last 1 m at both ends”, citing Bell 1999, which does not say so.

Coil papers side by side

Eight documents on the coil: five in the table below (three read from the PDFs in references/babar_coil/, the TDR chapter 9 from PostScript and O'Connor et al. 1999 from its Word manuscript), plus the 1997 MT-15 paper, the 1997 technical specification of the solenoid and a 2022 JLab risk assessment that reprints O'Connor's tables. The TDR (March 1995) is the BABARv10 coil; the 1996 paper is a revised single-layer design from the same year; the two 1999 coil papers describe the coil as built and agree. O'Connor also gives the pre-test design values. The 1997 MT-15 table mixes the IEEE 1996 single-layer geometry and inductance with the as-built turns and current; see the README there.
