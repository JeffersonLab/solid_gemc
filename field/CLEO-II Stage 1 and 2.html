CLEO-II for SoLID: stage 1 and two options for stage 2

Key findings

The plug does most of the field cancellation and causes the force imbalance. The correction coil trims what’s left and doesn’t affect the force, but cooling caps it at roughly what CLEOv11 already uses. Steel near the downstream coil end (the nose front, plus the inner part of the wall if there is one) balances the force. The donut and back plates control the leak with almost no effect on the force.

  • The upstream plug does most of the field cancellation at the target. It brings Bz(0, −350) from 670 G to 41 G, and the spread over z −350 ± 30 from about 690 G to 96 G. The correction coil trims the remaining 41 G to zero.
  • The plug also unbalances the coil. It pulls the main coil about 44 t upstream. The correction coil's current does not change the coil force (identical at 100 and 996 A/cm²).
  • Steel closest to the coil ends (z = ±173.8) sets the force. Downstream, the nose front balances the plug, together with the inner part of the nose wall if there is one.
  • The correction coil cannot replace the plug. Air cooling caps it at about 100–150 A/cm²; CLEOv11 runs at . Without a plug it would need about 266 kA-turns (about 300 kW) and still leave the field gradient across the target. So the plug should be the smallest one that leaves about 40 G for an air-cooled coil; a more efficient coil shape allows a smaller plug.
  • The donut and back plates hardly affect the force (at most 1.7 t) but control the leak: the plates shield the downstream detector region, the donut the outside (r ≳ 250 cm).
  • With all new downstream steel inside r 60 cm (from 2026-10-30), CLEOv11 balances the coil with a short nose plus a bridge, a steel tube joining the nose to the back plates (their real bore, r 40 cm). The bridge runs fully saturated. The field at the photosensor point (215, 344) is lowest with the shortest nose the 2° bore allows: .
  • CLEOv11's force balance depends on its saturated bridge; CLEOv12's hardly depends on the steel.
  • The nose wall is worth about 17.6 t of force, and a longer solid front can replace it (CLEOv12). Without the plates, dropping the wall lowers the leak far downstream but raises it just behind the nose.

  • Radial force is reasonable and set by the bore field, not by the endcap. Bz just inside the winding at z 0 is 1.47 T, so B²/2μ0 · r ≈ 1.31 MN/m/rad; the field falls across the winding, giving the 1.26 found. It is uniform along the coil (1.24–1.28 MN/m/rad by section). The inner layer carries about 3.5× the outer, because the field drops from about 1.47 T to 0.7 T across it and to about 0 across the outer one. The page's estimate of about 30 MPa hoop tension in the shell scales to about 31–32 MPa, well under the roughly 100 MPa aluminum yield it quotes.
  • The end sections are compressed about 20 % more than on the CLEO page: 364–367 t per end with the stage-2 steel, 392 t in stage 1, against about 300 t there (different iron). The page's 8 MPa compressive stress scales to about 10 MPa. Use about 365–390 t per end for the mechanical check.

CLEOv11 · Endcap bridge (r ≤ 60 cm)

With all new steel inside r 60 cm, the nose alone cannot balance the coil while the back plates sit 300 cm downstream. A bridge, a steel tube with outer radius 60 cm from the nose back face to the first back plate, closes that gap; the plates are modelled at their real bore, r 40 cm. Two parameters: where the solid nose ends and the bridge bore. A smaller bore pulls the coil downstream (about 2 t per cm of bore). The field at the photosensor point (215, 344) depends mainly on the nose length, so the shortest nose the 2° bore allows is best. The highlighted row is CLEOv11.

How these numbers were obtained

Corrected steel model. The original input used rhogam = 1e-6, copied from CLEO_E_03. That froze the steel's permeability near its starting value, so saturated steel was modeled as far too permeable, and the coil force came out up to 26 t wrong. The three designs use rhogam = 0.02; the scans used 0.08 (POISSON's default). Both let the permeability settle and give the same results, but 0.08 can swing back and forth in fully saturated steel (the CLEOv11 bridge) and then never meets POISSON's stopping test; 0.02 converges there too.

  • Every point is a full AUTOMESH → POISSON → SF7 → FORCE solve with Poisson Superfish 7.18 under Wine. Scans ran in parallel scratch copies; each design (CLEOv10, CLEOv11, CLEOv12) was then run in its own directory. Field-line figures are WSFPLOT exports of those runs.
  • Bz at the target is linear in the correction-coil current (checked to 0.001 G), so two solves per coil shape give the cancelling current.
  • Constraints: the 25° plug bore and 6.5° nose cone keep 6.5°–25° clear, and the nose bore stays above 2° (r ≥ 20 cm). From 2026-10-30 all new downstream steel stays inside r 60 cm. The correction coil is air cooled, at most 20 cm long, and sits on its support ring at r ≥ 38.94 cm. Coil force tolerance ±2 t. The target volume is z = −350 ± 30 cm, r ≤ 2 cm.
  • Steel is 1010 at 7.87 g/cm³. Copper loss assumes 70 % fill at 20 °C.
